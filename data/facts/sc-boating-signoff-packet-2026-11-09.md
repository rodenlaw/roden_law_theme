| 1 | S.C. Code § 50-21-112 | boating under the influence (BUI) offense | It is unlawful to operate a moving motorized water device or a water device under sail on the waters of this State while under the influence of alcohol, any other drug, or both, to the extent that the person's faculties to operate are materially and appreciably impaired. The offense is impairment-based: the section sets no blood-alcohol (BAC) level at which operating is per se illegal (the 0.08 figure is an evidentiary inference under § 50-21-114, not an element). Misdemeanor penalties: first offense a $200 fine or 48 hours to 30 days (48 hours of public service in lieu); second offense $2,000 to $5,000 and 48 hours to 1 year; third offense $3,500 to $6,000 and 60 days to 3 years. The department must also bar operation of any water device for 6 months (first), 1 year (second) and 2 years (third conviction); priors count within 10 years; ADSAP and a boating safety course are required before reinstatement. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 2 | S.C. Code § 50-21-114 | BUI implied consent, chemical testing and BAC inferences | A person who operates a water device is deemed to consent to breath, blood or urine testing if arrested for a BUI offense; a breath test is offered first. In a criminal prosecution the BAC gives rise to these inferences: 0.05% or less, conclusively presumed not under the influence; more than 0.05% but less than 0.08%, no inference either way; 0.08% or more, it may be inferred that the person was under the influence. Refusal: no test may be given, the department must suspend the operating privilege for 180 days, and refusal is admissible at the trial of the charged offense. A coroner must order a blood test within 48 hours of notification of a boating fatality. The section does not make 0.08% a per se offense and says nothing about civil adverse inferences. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 3 | S.C. Code § 50-21-113 | BUI causing great bodily injury, death, property damage or other injury | Operating or being in actual control of a moving water device under the influence and causing great bodily injury or death of another is a felony: great bodily injury, mandatory $5,000 to $10,000 fine and 30 days to 15 years; death, $10,000 to $25,000 and 1 to 25 years; no suspension or probation. Causing property damage or injury short of great bodily injury is a misdemeanor: a fine of not less than $500 or up to 30 days, or both. Privilege suspended for any imprisonment term plus 3 years (death or great bodily injury) or 1 year (property damage or lesser injury). 'Great bodily injury' means injury creating a substantial risk of death or causing serious, permanent disfigurement or protracted loss or impairment of a bodily member or organ. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 4 | S.C. Code § 50-21-130 | duty to render aid, exchange information and report a boating accident | The operator of a vessel involved in a collision, accident or other casualty must, if he can do so without serious danger to his own vessel, crew or passengers, render practical or necessary assistance and give his name, address and vessel identification in writing to anyone injured and to the owner of damaged property. Failing to stop or comply is a misdemeanor (30 days to 1 year or $100 to $5,000, or both) when injury or damage results short of great bodily injury; a felony when great bodily injury results (30 days to 10 years and $5,000 to $10,000); a felony when death results (1 to 25 years and $10,000 to $25,000). A person who gratuitously and in good faith renders assistance at the scene is not liable for civil damages for it. For a reportable accident (defined in § 50-21-10(21)) the operator or owner must file a full description with SCDNR within 48 hours; for death, loss of consciousness or serious bodily injury the owner or operator must notify scdnr immediately. The report is for the department's information: an injured person, the person alleged to have caused the injury, their attorney, personal representative or an insurer may obtain a copy, but no report or statement in it is admissible as evidence in a civil trial (the fact of filing is admissible only to show compliance). Suspensions: 2 years for not rendering assistance, 1 year for not reporting, and no reinstatement until a boating safety course is completed. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 5 | S.C. Code § 50-21-10 | Title 50 boating definitions: reportable boating accident, vessel, water device, waters of the State | A 'reportable boating accident' is an accident, collision or other casualty involving a vessel subject to the chapter that results in loss of life; injury causing loss of consciousness, the need for medical treatment, the need to carry a person from the scene, or disability preventing normal duties beyond the day of the casualty; or physical damage to property (including vessels) in excess of the minimum amount set by the U.S. Coast Guard for reportable accidents. The dollar figure is not in the S.C. statute: it incorporates the federal minimum, which 33 C.F.R. § 173.55(a)(3) sets at damage totalling $2,000 or more (read 2026-11-09 on ecfr.gov). 'Waters of the State' are waters within the State's territorial limits but not private lakes or ponds. 'Vessel' is every description of watercraft used or capable of being used for transportation on water (other than a federally regulated seaplane); 'water device' adds water skis, aquaplanes, surfboards and similar devices; 'personal watercraft' is a jet-pump vessel, usually under 16 feet, ridden rather than sat in. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 6 | S.C. Code § 50-21-110 | negligent operation of a water device | No person may operate a water device in a negligent manner. Negligent operation includes, but is not limited to, operating at more than idle speed in a no wake zone, failing to keep a proper lookout for other boats or persons, operating too fast for conditions on the water, racing, or pulling a skier through a designated swimming area. Misdemeanor: $50 to $200 or up to 30 days per violation; three convictions in 5 years require a boating safety course, with the privilege suspended until it is completed. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 7 | S.C. Code § 50-21-111 | reckless operation of a water device | Operating a water device in a manner indicating a wilful or wanton disregard for the safety of persons or property is reckless operation; examples include weaving through congested traffic above idle speed, jumping another vessel's wake within 200 feet of it, crossing a vessel's path or wake when visibility around it is obstructed, and holding a collision course and swerving away at close range. Misdemeanor: $100 to $200 or up to 30 days; a boating safety course is required; a second conviction within 5 years brings a 90-day suspension, then suspension until the course is completed. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 8 | S.C. Code § 50-21-115 | reckless homicide by operation of a boat | When a death ensues within three years as a proximate result of injury received by operating a boat in reckless disregard of the safety of others, the operator is guilty of reckless homicide: $1,000 to $5,000 or up to 10 years, or both. A person convicted of reckless homicide, involuntary manslaughter, manslaughter or murder in the operation of a boat must be barred by the court from operating any boat in the State for not more than 5 years. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 9 | S.C. Code § 50-21-95 | boating safety certificate required to operate (boater education) | It is unlawful to operate on the waters of this State a vessel powered by an engine of 10 horsepower or more (or equivalent), a personal watercraft or a specialty propcraft unless the operator was born on or before July 1, 2007; holds (or is documented by SCDNR as holding) a South Carolina boating safety certificate; holds a U.S. Coast Guard vessel operator's license or merchant mariner credential (expiration date irrelevant); is a nonresident with another state's certificate; is operating a rental from a rental business with a valid boat rental safety certificate (valid 30 days under § 50-21-90(C)); or is accompanied by a person at least 18 years old who meets one of the first five criteria. A violation is not a criminal offense: a fine of $50 to $300, no court costs, no custodial arrest except on a failure-to-appear or failure-to-pay warrant, and no SCDNR or SLED record. Effective August 18, 2023 (2023 Act No. 74, § 3). Chapter 21 sets no minimum age to operate: the same act (§ 5) deleted the former § 50-21-870(B)(9) restriction on operators younger than sixteen. Earlier statements of a sixteen-year minimum or a birth-date cutoff other than July 1, 2007 are out of date. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 10 | S.C. Code § 50-21-870 | personal watercraft and boating safety rules (life jackets, hours, idle-speed distances) | No person may operate a personal watercraft or specialty propcraft unless everyone aboard wears a Coast Guard-approved Type I, II, III or V personal flotation device, or operate a Class 'A' motorboat (under sixteen feet) unless every person under 12 aboard wears one; operate a PWC or specialty propcraft after sunset or before sunrise; operate one without a working self-circling device or lanyard engine cutoff (lanyard attached to the operator); on the named lakes (Greenwood, Hartwell, Jocassee, Keowee, Marion, Monticello, Murray, Robinson, Russell, Secession, Thurmond, Wateree, Fishing Creek and Parr reservoirs, and a stretch of the Savannah River) exceed idle speed within 100 feet of a wharf, dock, bulkhead or pier or 50 feet of a moored or anchored vessel or person in the water; on all other waters exceed idle speed within 50 feet of a moored or anchored vessel, wharf, dock, bulkhead, pier or person in the water, or within 100 yards of the Atlantic coastline (Lake Moultrie excepted); tow a skier with a PWC without a wide-angle mirror or an observer; or wake surf above idle speed within 200 feet of a moored vessel, dock or person in the water. An owner or person in charge who knowingly permits a violation also violates the section. Misdemeanor: $50 to $300. Private waters are exempt. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 11 | S.C. Code § 50-21-120 | vessel owner's liability for negligent operation; boat livery duties and liability | The owner of a vessel is liable for injury or damage caused by its negligent operation, whether the negligence is a statutory violation or a failure to use ordinary care, but only if the vessel is used with the owner's express or implied consent or is in the possession of a person or organization legally responsible for it; operation by a member of the owner's household is presumed to be with the owner's knowledge and consent. Other persons' liability is not reduced. A boat livery may not let a vessel depart unless it is in sound and safe operating condition, validly registered, properly numbered and equipped as § 50-21-610 requires, and the livery owner is liable for damage or injury resulting directly from that failure; but the livery is not liable as owner for negligent operation of a rented vessel, and the renter-operator is liable as owner. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
| 12 | S.C. Code § 50-21-710 | regulatory markers; operating in a prohibited area is negligent operation | Operating a vessel, watercraft or water device within a prohibited area marked under the section is negligent operation unless its seriousness makes it reckless operation (§ 50-21-710(C)); operating other than as regulatory markers (including no wake zones) prescribe is unlawful (D); all existing no wake zones are deemed established under the section (F). The statute defines a criminal offense; it does not itself say that a violation is negligence per se in a civil case, and causation must still be shown. | ☐ approve ☐ correct |
