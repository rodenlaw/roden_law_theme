# How Much Does Georgia Workers' Comp Pay? Weekly Checks, Body-Part Ratings and the Deadlines That Protect Them

Last reviewed:

If a work injury keeps you out of work for more than seven days [VERIFY — attorney (V2-12, resolves with V2-3): seven-day waiting period from the State Board's FAQ, not yet traced to statute], Georgia workers' compensation pays you **two-thirds of your average weekly wage**, up to a weekly maximum set by statute (**O.C.G.A. § 34-9-261**).

According to the State Board of Workers' Compensation's [Workers' Compensation Law FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-law-faqs), that maximum is **$800 a week** for an accident on or after July 1, 2023. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-1): $800 maximum and its July 1, 2023 effective date from the State Board's Workers' Compensation Law FAQ, https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-law-faqs, read 2026-11-03; the GA pack claim for § 34-9-261 says to confirm the current figure before publishing any number, and no 2024–2026 amendment has been checked.]

When you are hurt on the job, the first question is usually the simplest one: how much will I be paid while I cannot work? This guide answers how much Georgia workers' comp pays, one benefit at a time, and shows you the two deadlines that can cost you the check entirely.

## Key Takeaways

- Georgia's weekly income benefit for total disability is two-thirds of your average weekly wage, up to the statutory maximum (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-261).
- You must miss more than seven days of work before weekly benefits are owed. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-12, resolves with V2-3)]
- If you go back to work at lower pay because of the injury, a reduced-earnings benefit may make up part of the difference (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-262).
- A permanent impairment to a body part listed in the statute is paid for a set number of weeks, such as 225 weeks for an arm or a leg (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-263).
- Comp does not pay for pain and suffering, and you generally cannot sue your employer, but you may have a separate claim against someone else who caused the injury (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-11).
- Report the injury to your employer within 30 days (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-80) and file your claim with the State Board in time (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-82), or you can lose these benefits.

## How much is the weekly check?

The weekly check while you cannot work at all is two-thirds of your average weekly wage before the injury, capped at the statutory maximum (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-261). Georgia calls this benefit **temporary total disability (TTD)**.

| Your average weekly wage | Two-thirds | What you are paid (at an $800 maximum) |
|---|---|---|
| $600 | $400 | $400 |
| $900 | $600 | $600 |
| $1,200 | $800 | $800 |
| $1,500 | $1,000 | $800 |

[VERIFY — attorney (V2-2): the table's last column depends on the $800 figure above; recompute if the maximum differs.]

The arithmetic matters most for higher earners. Once two-thirds of your wage passes the maximum, every extra dollar you earned before the injury makes no difference to the check.

Your average weekly wage is based on what you earned before the injury. If your pay varied, or you had a second job, the figure the insurer uses is worth checking line by line.

According to the State Board of Workers' Compensation's [2026 Annual Report](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/document/publication/annual-report-2026/download), insurers, self-insurers and group funds reported 28,097 indemnity claims during calendar year 2025 and $871,816,200 in indemnity benefits. Indemnity claims are the ones that pay income benefits rather than medical bills alone, so a wage figure that is off by even a little repeats itself in every check.

### When the first check comes

According to the State Board of Workers' Compensation's [Workers' Compensation Law FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-law-faqs), your first check should be mailed within 21 days after the first day you missed work. Weekly benefits are owed only if you are unable to work for more than seven days, and if you miss more than 21 consecutive days, you are also paid for the first week. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-3): the seven-day waiting period, the 21-day first payment and the first-week rule are from the State Board's Workers' Compensation Law FAQ (URL above), read 2026-11-03; trace to O.C.G.A. § 34-9-220 and § 34-9-221 before publishing, neither of which is in the GA pack or on the Verified table.]

## What if you go back to work at lower pay?

If your doctor releases you to light duty and you can only earn less than you did before, Georgia pays a reduced benefit called **temporary partial disability (TPD)** (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-262). It is based on the difference between your old and new earnings.

According to the State Board of Workers' Compensation's [FAQ on Georgia benefits](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-law-faqs), TPD cannot exceed $533 a week for an accident on or after July 1, 2023, and it is paid for no more than 350 weeks from the date of injury. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-4): $533, its effective date and the 350-week limit from the State Board's Workers' Compensation Law FAQ (URL above), read 2026-11-03; § 34-9-262 is not in the GA pack or on the Verified table.]

Keep every pay stub from the lighter job. The reduced benefit is only as accurate as the earnings records behind it.

## What is a body-part rating worth?

A body-part rating is worth a set number of weeks of **permanent partial disability (PPD)** benefits, and the number depends on the body part (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-263). When your treatment ends and you are left with a lasting impairment, the authorized treating doctor gives you that rating.

| Body part (total loss or loss of use) | Weeks under the schedule |
|---|---|
| Arm | 225 |
| Leg | 225 |
| Hand | 160 |
| Eye | 150 |
| Foot | 135 |

Source: O.C.G.A. § 34-9-263(c).

A partial loss is paid in proportion to the rating. As an example, a 10% rating to the arm is 10% of 225 weeks, or 22.5 weeks of benefits. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-5): confirm that PPD is paid at the claimant's TTD rate (rating × scheduled weeks × weekly rate) under § 34-9-263(b), and that the doctor rates under the AMA Guides, fifth edition, as the State Board's FAQ states; neither point is in the GA pack.]

Two rules from the same section affect when the money arrives. PPD is payable whether or not the injury costs you any earnings, and it is not paid for weeks in which you are receiving TTD or TPD benefits (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-263(b)).

## What workers' comp does not pay

Georgia workers' compensation is your **exclusive remedy against your employer** (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-11). That means you generally cannot sue your employer for the injury, and comp does not pay for pain and suffering or award punitive damages.

You can still sue someone else. If a driver from another company hit your work vehicle, or a defective machine caused the injury, you may have a separate injury claim against that third party, where pain and suffering is recoverable. The employer or its insurer may have a lien on that recovery for benefits it paid, but only if you have been fully and completely compensated (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-11.1(b)). ## The two deadlines that protect your check

Georgia gives you two deadlines: tell your employer within 30 days, and file your claim with the State Board within the time limits of O.C.G.A. § 34-9-82. Every benefit on this page can be lost to a missed deadline.

| Step | Deadline | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Tell your employer about the injury | Within 30 days | O.C.G.A. § 34-9-80 |
| File your claim (Form WC-14) with the State Board | One year from the injury; or one year from the last remedial treatment your employer furnished; or two years from the last weekly benefit payment | O.C.G.A. § 34-9-82 |

There is no two-years-from-injury rule for a workers' compensation claim. The two-year deadline you may have heard about is the one for personal injury lawsuits (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33; see our guide to [Georgia's personal injury filing deadline](/resources/georgia-statute-of-limitations/)), not for comp. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q:** How much does Georgia workers' comp pay per week?

**A:** Two-thirds of your average weekly wage, up to the weekly maximum set by O.C.G.A. § 34-9-261. The State Board of Workers' Compensation states that maximum as $800 a week for an accident on or after July 1, 2023. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-8, resolves with V2-1): same figure as the opening section.] If two-thirds of your wage comes in under the maximum, you are paid the two-thirds figure; the table under "How much is the weekly check?" works through four wage levels.

**Q:** Is there a waiting period before Georgia workers' comp pays?

**A:** Yes. Weekly benefits are owed only if you are unable to work for more than seven days, and if you miss more than 21 consecutive days you are also paid for the first week. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-9, resolves with V2-3): same source and statute trace as "When the first check comes".] Keep a written record of every day you miss, so the insurer's start date and first payment can be checked against it.

**Q:** Do I get paid if I go back to work at a lower-paying job?

**A:** You may. Temporary partial disability benefits under O.C.G.A. § 34-9-262 make up part of the difference between what you earned before the injury and what you earn now. [VERIFY — attorney (V2-10, resolves with V2-4): § 34-9-262 not in the GA pack.] Keep every pay stub from the lighter job, because the benefit is calculated from the gap between your old earnings and your new ones.

**Q:** How is a permanent impairment paid in Georgia?

**A:** The authorized treating doctor gives an impairment rating, and Georgia pays for a set number of weeks depending on the body part, for example 225 weeks for an arm or a leg and 160 weeks for a hand (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-263). A partial loss is paid in proportion to the rating.

**Q:** Can I get pain and suffering from Georgia workers' comp?

**A:** No. Workers' compensation is the exclusive remedy against your employer and does not pay for pain and suffering (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-11). If someone other than your employer caused the injury, such as a negligent driver or the maker of a defective machine, you may have a separate claim against them that can include it.

**Q:** How long do I have to file a Georgia workers' comp claim?

**A:** Report the injury to your employer within 30 days (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-80). File your claim with the State Board within one year of the injury, one year from the last remedial treatment your employer furnished, or two years from the last weekly benefit payment (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-82).
