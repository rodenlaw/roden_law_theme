### Brief for `pi-content-writer`

Write a two-state reference guide at `/resources/georgia-vs-south-carolina-helmet-laws/` that
answers, in the first 60 words and before any qualification, who is legally required to wear a
helmet in each state — Georgia requires a helmet of every motorcycle rider and passenger under
**O.C.G.A. § 40-6-315**; South Carolina requires one only of riders and passengers **under 21**
under **S.C. Code § 56-5-3660** — then gives the facts in a two-column GA/SC comparison table
(motorcycle, moped/scooter, bicycle, e-bike, ATV and golf cart rows; age thresholds; DOT
compliance; eye-protection and face-shield duties where the statute states them; penalty) and
devotes its second half to the question readers actually arrive with: what helmet non-use does,
and does not do, to an injury claim in each state. Handle that section with care — **neither
§ 40-6-315 nor § 56-5-3660 says anything about evidence or damages**, so state plainly what the
statutes do say, frame the comparative-fault consequence as an argument insurers make rather
than a rule the code imposes, route it through the verified comparative-fault rules only
(Georgia: recover if **less than 50%** at fault, O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33; South Carolina: recover if
**less than 51%**, *Nelson v. Concrete Supply Co.* — **never** cite S.C. Code § 15-38-15 for the
plaintiff's bar), and mark any statement that a helmet-use fact is admissible, inadmissible or
automatically reduces recovery with an inline `[VERIFY — attorney]` tag rather than asserting
it; the closest analogue on this site, the car-seat admissibility rule, was wrong on three
surfaces of the best-performing resource on the domain (`KNOWLEDGE-BASE-PLAN-rodenlaw.md`
§6.1). Only `O.C.G.A. § 40-6-315` and `S.C. Code § 56-5-3660` may be asserted as verified; every
other section you want — bicycle helmets for under-16s in Georgia, Class III e-bike helmet
rules, e-scooter rules, and any municipal ordinance — is **not** on the tracker's Verified table
and must be either left out or traced yourself to primary text (scstatehouse.gov for SC,
codes.findlaw.com for GA — Justia 403s; see `docs/statute-verification-tracker.md` §Method) and
tagged `[VERIFY — attorney]` with the URL you read; do **not** take any statute from the GA or
SC law packs, which are unsigned. Before you draft, read the 20 pages listed in the appendix
below and reconcile what they already say — several assert the evidentiary consequence flatly,
`/practice-areas/bicycle-accident-lawyers/` says South Carolina "has no statewide helmet law"
while `/practice-areas/motorcycle-accident-lawyers/` correctly states the under-21 rule, and
`/practice-areas/electric-scooter-accident-lawyers/` asserts that "some South Carolina
municipalities require helmets" with no source — and list every divergence you find at the end
of your hand-off so a claim-class sweep can follow; this page is meant to become the one place
the firm states this law. House rules: statewide only, no city or county framing anywhere in
the title, H1, slug or headings (a rule page titled for a place is a doorway page wearing a hat
— `KNOWLEDGE-BASE-PLAN-rodenlaw.md` §10.4); 1,500–2,000 words; answer-first, second person,
short paragraphs, plain language; strict citation format `O.C.G.A. § X-X-XX` and `S.C. Code
§ XX-X-XXX` with no "Ann."; every figure carries its source and a snapshot date, and any figure
you cannot source comes out (`plays/2026-10.json` E17); inline prose attribution, never a `>`
blockquote; 8–13 internal links with anchors that name the destination's topic — link up to
`/practice-areas/motorcycle-accident-lawyers/`, `/practice-areas/bicycle-accident-lawyers/`,
`/resources/georgia-vs-south-carolina-comparative-negligence/` and
