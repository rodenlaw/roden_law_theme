### Why both pages clear the law-pack gate

Both packs are unsigned (`plays/2026-10.json` E5), so **no new claim may cite their
authorities**. Neither page needs them. Each page's one load-bearing statute is on the Verified
table of `docs/statute-verification-tracker.md`:

| Statute | Pages citing it today | Verified in |
|---|---:|---|
| `SC 56-5-3660` | 13 | 2026-09-03 pass 3 (#109) |
| `GA 40-6-315` | 7 | 2026-09-03 pass 4 |

and the pass log records pass 4 as clean with "the helmet and crosswalk duties … all stated
correctly, several of them word-for-word from the statute". Each page therefore ships on the
same footing as posts 5352/5353: every statement traced to verified primary text, nothing
resting on an unsigned pack.

### Shared brief for `pi-content-writer` (run once per entry)

Write a single-state reference page for the state named in the entry, answering in the first
60 words and before any qualification who is legally required to wear a helmet there — Georgia:
every motorcycle rider and passenger, **O.C.G.A. § 40-6-315**; South Carolina: riders and
passengers **under 21**, **S.C. Code § 56-5-3660** — then give the facts in a single-column
table by vehicle (motorcycle, moped/scooter, bicycle, e-bike, ATV, golf cart: who must wear one,
age threshold, DOT compliance, eye-protection or face-shield duty where the statute states it,
penalty), and devote the second half to the question readers actually arrive with: what helmet
non-use does, and does not do, to an injury claim in that state. Handle that section with care —
**neither § 40-6-315 nor § 56-5-3660 says anything about evidence or damages** — so state
plainly what the statute does say, frame the comparative-fault consequence as an argument
insurers make rather than a rule the code imposes, route it through the verified
comparative-fault rule for that state only (see the pair table; **never** cite S.C. Code
§ 15-38-15 for the plaintiff's bar), and mark any statement that a helmet-use fact is
admissible, inadmissible or automatically reduces recovery with an inline `[VERIFY — attorney]`
tag rather than asserting it; the closest analogue on this site, the car-seat admissibility
rule, was wrong on three surfaces of the best-performing resource on the domain
(`KNOWLEDGE-BASE-PLAN-rodenlaw.md` §6.1). Only that state's verified statute may be asserted as
verified; every other section you want — Georgia's bicycle helmet rule for riders under 16,
South Carolina's absence of a statewide bicycle helmet law, Class III e-bike helmet rules,
e-scooter rules, and any municipal ordinance — is **not** on the tracker's Verified table and
must be either left out or traced yourself to primary text (scstatehouse.gov for SC,
codes.findlaw.com for GA — Justia 403s; see `docs/statute-verification-tracker.md` §Method) and
tagged `[VERIFY — attorney]` with the URL you read; do **not** take any statute from the GA or
SC law packs, which are unsigned. Include one short section, under 150 words, headed "How the
rule differs across the state line", that states the other state's headline rule in one
sentence and links to the sister page; do not restate the other state's law beyond that. Before
you draft, read the pages in the appendix that concern your state and reconcile what they
already say — several assert the evidentiary consequence flatly,
`/practice-areas/bicycle-accident-lawyers/` says South Carolina "has no statewide helmet law"
while `/practice-areas/motorcycle-accident-lawyers/` correctly states the under-21 rule, and
`/practice-areas/electric-scooter-accident-lawyers/` asserts that "some South Carolina
municipalities require helmets" with no source — and list every divergence you find at the end
of your hand-off so a claim-class sweep can follow; this page is meant to become the one place
the firm states this state's helmet law. House rules: statewide only, no city or county framing
anywhere in the title, H1, slug or headings (a rule page titled for a place is a doorway page
wearing a hat — `KNOWLEDGE-BASE-PLAN-rodenlaw.md` §10.4); 1,200–1,600 words; answer-first,
second person, short paragraphs, plain language; strict citation format `O.C.G.A. § X-X-XX` or
`S.C. Code § XX-X-XXX` with no "Ann."; every figure carries its source and a snapshot date, and
any figure you cannot source comes out (`plays/2026-10.json` E17); inline prose attribution,
never a `>` blockquote; 6–10 internal links with anchors that name the destination's topic —
link up to `/practice-areas/motorcycle-accident-lawyers/`,
`/practice-areas/bicycle-accident-lawyers/`, the sister helmet page, and for SC also
`/resources/south-carolina-personal-injury-faq/` and `/south-carolina-motorcycle-accident-lawyer/`,
for GA also `/resources/georgia-vs-south-carolina-comparative-negligence/` — and verify every
path returns 200 before you use it (all of the above did on 2026-09-19; the sister page will not
until it publishes, so link it last and note it); six or more FAQs in the `**Q:** … / **A:** …`
bold-prefix format (the theme maps these into FAQPage structured data, so a wrong answer
