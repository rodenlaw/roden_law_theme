# I-26 / I-95 Corridor Report — media kit

Report: https://rodenlaw.com/resources/i-26-i-95-corridor-report/
Dataset: https://rodenlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/i26-i95-corridor-fatal-crashes-2020-2024.csv

**Read the "What not to say" section before sending anything.** A pitch that
overstates the findings gets the report discredited on the first fact-check, and
the report's only asset is that it survives one.

---

## The one-paragraph version

Between 2020 and 2024, 323 fatal crashes on Interstates 26 and 95 in Georgia and
South Carolina killed 374 people. A large truck was involved in 109 of them. The
share is not uniform: **45% of fatal crashes on Georgia's I-95 involved a large
truck, against 27% on South Carolina's I-26** — and in several rural counties it
runs higher still. Analysis of NHTSA's Fatality Analysis Reporting System, with
the crash-level dataset and the analysis code published alongside.

## Why a reporter should care

- **It is checkable.** The dataset and the script are published. A reporter can
  rerun it, or take the CSV and cut it their own way. Most law-firm "studies"
  cannot survive that and do not offer it.
- **It is local.** Every county on both corridors is in the data. The per-market
  cuts below are already done.
- **The headline is counterintuitive in at least two markets** — see Charleston
  and the Pee Dee.

## Per-market angles

Each of these is a different story, not the same story with the place name
swapped. Pitch the one that belongs to the outlet.

### Pee Dee / Florence — the strongest local number in the study
**59% of fatal crashes on I-95 here involved a large truck** — the highest share of
any market on either corridor, above Savannah and more than double Charleston.
Dillon 8 of 14 (57%), Florence 7 of 12 (58%). Rural I-95, 27 fatal crashes, 31
deaths.
*Angle: the deadliest truck corridor in the study runs through the Pee Dee, and
almost nobody is talking about it.*

### Savannah / coastal Georgia — the headline market
**45% truck involvement on Georgia's I-95**, the highest of the three corridor
segments. McIntosh County 6 of 10 (60%), Camden 6 of 12 (50%), Liberty 5 of 10
(50%), Chatham 9 of 21 (43%). 71 fatal crashes, 83 deaths.
*Angle: the port corridor's freight traffic shows up in the fatal-crash record,
and it concentrates in the rural counties south of Savannah rather than in the
city.*

### Charleston — the counterintuitive one
**23% truck involvement, the LOWEST of any market** — despite the Port of
Charleston. 74 fatal crashes, 83 deaths, 17 truck-involved. The exception is
Dorchester County at 6 of 13 (46%).
*Angle: the port city has the lowest truck share on its interstates in the study.
Worth asking why — and Dorchester's 46% suggests the freight risk sits inland of
the port, not beside it. Do not force a scarier framing than the data supports.*

### Columbia / Midlands — the volume market
102 fatal crashes and 120 deaths, the most of any market, at a 27% truck share.
**Orangeburg County alone had 37 fatal crashes** — more than any other single
county in the study. Newberry 5 of 11 (45%).
*Angle: the I-26 Midlands stretch carries the highest fatal-crash count in the
study; trucks are a smaller share of a bigger problem.*

### Upstate / Spartanburg
**43% truck involvement**, 30 fatal crashes, 39 deaths. Spartanburg 8 of 19 (42%),
on the I-26 freight run toward the inland port.
*Angle: I-26's Upstate stretch looks more like coastal Georgia than like the rest
of South Carolina.*

### Trucking and logistics trade press
Corridor-level comparison across two states, with the tractor-trailer-only subset
reported separately (39% / 25% / 18%). **68% of truck-involved fatal crashes were
on rural stretches**, not in the urban sections where volumes are highest.
*Angle: rural concentration, and a two-state comparison no single-state dataset
produces.*

## What not to say

These are the lines that would get the report taken apart, and every one of them
is a claim the report explicitly declines to make.

| Do not say | Because |
|---|---|
| "the deadliest highway in Georgia/South Carolina" | The report ranks corridor **segments by truck share**, not roads by danger. It makes no deadliest claim. |
| "trucks caused X% of fatal crashes" | FARS records **which vehicles were present, not who was at fault**. Involvement is not fault. This is the single most likely misreading. |
| "X crashes on I-26 last year" | These are **fatal crashes only**. Total crashes are far higher and are not in this dataset. |
| "more dangerous per mile" | These are **counts, not rates**. Without vehicle-miles-travelled by segment, a busier road is not a more hazardous one. |
| Any 2024 figure as settled | FARS is **republished as case investigations close**. Describe 2024 as the current annual file. |

If a reporter asks for something the data cannot answer — fault, injury crashes,
crash rates per mile — the honest answer is that this dataset does not contain it
and here is what would. That answer builds more credit than a stretched one.

## Contacts — verify before sending

**No individual reporter names or addresses are listed here on purpose.** Bylines
and beats move, and a pitch sent to someone who left the outlet two years ago is
worse than no pitch. Before sending, confirm the current transportation, public
safety or investigations reporter from the outlet's own staff page or a recent
byline on a comparable story.

the *Post and Courier* for its tri-county dangerous-intersections analysis, and
*Live 5 News* for its July 2026 piece on SCDOT's Charleston County road-safety
data. Both are cited in Roden Law pages, which is a reason to approach them
straight rather than cute.
