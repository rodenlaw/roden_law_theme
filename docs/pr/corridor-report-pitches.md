# Pitch drafts — I-26 / I-95 Corridor Report

Send from a named person at the firm, not a generic address. Attach nothing; the
report and the CSV are both public URLs and an attachment from an unknown sender
is a spam filter's favourite meal.

Every draft below leads with the outlet's own number, not the firm's name. If the
first line is about Roden Law it will not be read.

## 5. Trade press — Transport Topics, FreightWaves, Overdrive
**Subject: Two-state corridor analysis: truck involvement in I-26/I-95 fatal crashes, 2020–2024**

Hello [NAME],

Published a corridor-level analysis of NHTSA FARS data for I-26 and I-95 across
Georgia and South Carolina, 2020–2024. Findings that may interest your readers:

- Truck involvement in fatal crashes varies sharply by segment: 45% on Georgia's
  I-95, 36% on South Carolina's I-95, 27% on South Carolina's I-26.
- The ordering holds under a tractor-trailer-only definition (39% / 25% / 18%),
  which we report alongside the broader GVWR>10,000 lb figure rather than choosing
  the more dramatic one.
- **68% of truck-involved fatal crashes were on rural stretches**, not the urban
  sections carrying the most traffic.
- Truck-involved fatal crashes averaged 1.22 deaths against 1.13 without.

The crash-level dataset and the analysis script are both published, so the numbers
are reproducible: https://rodenlaw.com/resources/i-26-i-95-corridor-report/

We are explicit in the report that FARS records vehicle presence rather than fault,
and that these are counts rather than per-mile rates.

## Follow-up

One follow-up after five working days, no more. Reply in the same thread, add one
thing that was not in the first email — most usefully the county-level cut for
that outlet's specific coverage area, which is in `market-cuts.json` and takes a
minute to pull.

If a reporter asks for something the data cannot answer, say so plainly and say
what would answer it. On a first approach, being the source that admits a limit is
worth more than the story you talked your way into.
