**Found 2026-08-25 while verifying a single statutory threshold.** South Carolina
enacted comprehensive tort and liquor-liability reform — **2025 Act No. 42
(H.3430)**, signed 12 May 2025, **effective 1 January 2026** — and nothing on this
site mentions it.

This is the South Carolina counterpart to the SB 68 audit, and the exposure is
larger.

## What Act 42 did

Verified against the South Carolina Legislature's own bill text and the current
Code:

| Section | Change |
|---|---|
| **§ 15-38-15** | Joint and several liability — **the alcohol exception was removed**, bringing dram-shop cases under ordinary comparative-fault rules |
| **§ 61-2-147** (new) | Where a DUI defendant and a licensed establishment are both liable, the licensee faces joint and several liability for **50% of the plaintiff's actual damages** |
| **§ 61-2-145** | Licensee liability-insurance requirements |
| **§ 61-6-2220** | Alcohol sales prohibitions |
| Title 61 Ch. 3 (new) | Mandatory alcohol-server training |
| — | Procedures for adding non-party tortfeasors to verdict forms |

**Applies to causes of action arising after 1 January 2026.** Injuries before that
date are governed by prior law, so existing content is not simply wrong — it is
incomplete, and it is wrong for anyone injured in the last eight months.

## What was fixed in this pass

Only the § 15-38-15 threshold, because that was bounded and verifiable:

`/blog/truck-accident-liability/` stated the rule as *"exceptions for defendants
**more than 50%** at fault."* The statute reads *"joint and several liability does
not apply to any defendant whose conduct is determined to be **less than fifty
percent** of the total fault."* Those differ at exactly 50%: a defendant at 50%
**is** jointly liable, and the page said otherwise. Corrected to the statutory
wording. `/resources/south-carolina-comparative-negligence/` already had it right.

The two-state comparative-negligence guide now quotes the statutory language
directly and notes the Act 42 amendment.

## What was not fixed, and why

**The dram-shop content is a project, not an edit.** Doing it properly means
reading the Act's operative text rather than a summary, deciding how to present a
rule that depends on whether the injury pre-dates 1 January 2026, and revising
about two dozen pages. Attempting that from a summary is how the seat-belt error
happened in the first place.

**Recommended shape**, in order:

1. Verify the operative text of § 61-2-147 and amended § 15-38-15 against the
   enrolled Act — not a client alert, not a news summary.
2. Build a fact base in `docs/briefs/`, in the same allowlist-and-forbidden-list
   form as the SB 68 brief. That format has now caught two errors.
3. Write one authoritative page on the 2026 changes, then correct the two dozen
   pages against it.

There is also a content opportunity here that the firm is currently missing
entirely: a two-state firm with an SC dram-shop practice, eight months into a
reform nobody has written about clearly, is well placed to own that ground.

## The pattern, stated once more

Two statutory changes, two states, both roughly a year old, **both found by
accident while doing something else**. Georgia's seat-belt reversal survived
sixteen months. South Carolina's tort reform has survived eight and counting.

Nothing on this site tracks whether a statutory change has reached the pages that
state the old rule. Until something does, the next one will be found the same way.
