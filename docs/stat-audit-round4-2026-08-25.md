# Claims assessment, round 4 — the remaining backlog

Round 3 established the method: **search for the source before removing a claim.**
This round applied it to what was left, and the result is mostly *not* removal.

## What was actually left

A re-scan of every published post after rounds 1–3 returned 133 claim-shaped
sentences across 98 pages. Splitting them by whether the subject is a **rankable
place** or a **hazard type** collapses that sharply:

| Class | Sentences | Pages | Disposition |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Hazard-type statements | 36 | 35 | **Leave** |
| Named-place rankings | 33 | 26 | Assess |

"Underride crashes are among the deadliest truck accident types", "intersections
are the most dangerous location for cyclists", "trench collapses are among the
deadliest hazards in construction" — these describe a mechanism, not a rankable
place. They are not falsifiable and they are not a liability. Earlier rounds
nearly swept them up; they should not be.

Of the 26 place-ranking pages, most are **table-of-contents lines** or **link
anchor text pointing at three roundup pages** — Columbia, Myrtle Beach and
Savannah. Those roundups are the load-bearing pages; the anchors inherit whatever
the roundups are worth.

## Verified true, and upgraded rather than removed

**"National safety studies have ranked I-95 as one of the most dangerous highways
in the entire country"** (`savannah-dangerous-highways-i16-i95-abercorn`).

True. FARS 2022–2024 puts I-95 **second of 222 interstate designations** by fatal
crashes — 924 crashes, 1,020 deaths, behind only I-10. The problem was never the
claim, it was "national safety studies", which names nothing a reader can check.
Replaced with the figure and the source.

## Removed

**"1 in 4 accidents classified as dangerous-level collisions"**
(`port-of-savannah-truck-routes`). *Dangerous-level collision* is not a recognised
crash classification anywhere — not in FARS, not in SCDPS or GDOT reporting. This
appears to be a mangled restatement of an earlier version of the same sentence,
which claimed 1 in 4 crashes caused serious injury or death. Neither version names
a retrievable source, and the current wording does not mean anything. Removed
along with the unsourced Savannah ranking attached to it.

## Left alone, and the reason is instructive

**Two claims survived because my evidence did not actually address them**, which
is worth recording as carefully as anything removed.

`dangerous-savannah-intersections` says Chatham County "consistently ranks among
the top five Georgia counties **for total traffic collisions**", citing GDOT. I
tested Chatham against FARS and found it sixth to eighth among Georgia counties
every year from 2020 to 2024 — and nearly corrected the page on that basis. But
**FARS counts deaths and the claim counts collisions.** Chatham is Georgia's
fifth-most-populous county; top-five by total crashes is entirely plausible and my
data says nothing about it. The claim names its agency and its metric, which is
more than most of what was removed this week.

`columbia-dangerous-intersections-roads` says I-20 is the most dangerous highway
in the Columbia area **"by crash volume"** — again all-severity, again not what
FARS measures. As it happens FARS agrees on the direction: I-20 carries the most
fatal crashes in Richland and Lexington counties, 35 against I-77's 31 and I-26's
30, 2020–2024. The margin is thinner than the superlative implies, but the claim
is qualified and not contradicted.

**The lesson, stated plainly: a claim is only falsified by evidence measuring the
same thing it measures.** Rounds 1 and 2 removed claims for being unverifiable;
round 3 found three of those were true; round 4 nearly removed two more on
evidence about a different metric entirely. Metric mismatch is the subtler failure
and it points the same direction — toward removing things that were fine.

## Still unsourced, flagged rather than removed

`columbia-dangerous-intersections-roads`: *"I-20 through Richland County produced
934 collisions, killing 5 people and injuring 260"* in a single unnamed year. That
is specific, all-severity, and cites nothing. It is exactly the shape of claim
that rounds 1 and 2 removed — but after this week, the right next step is a
records request to SCDPS for the underlying figure, not a deletion. Flagged for
whoever picks that up.
