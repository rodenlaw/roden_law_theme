A US-17 truck accident in Glynn County is a collision between a commercial tractor-trailer and another vehicle on the US-17 / GA-25 (Ocean Highway) corridor — typically near the Broadfield community between Brunswick and Darien, where the two-lane U.S. highway meets cross-roads like Grants Ferry Road (SR-99). Georgia law gives you 2 years to file under O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33. If you or someone you love was hit by an 80,000-pound rig on US-17 — threading the Broadfield curves with summer tourists — you are not dealing with an ordinary fender-bender. You are facing a national trucking company, its insurer, and a clock that is already running. Key Takeaways

  • Georgia gives you 2 years to file a personal injury lawsuit from the date of a US-17 truck crash under O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33 — and carriers may lawfully discard inspection reports after three months and driver logs after six, while ECM and dashcam data have no federal retention rule, so a preservation letter should go out early.
  • If you are 50% or more at fault, you recover nothing under Georgia's modified comparative-negligence rule at O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33 — trucking-defense lawyers know this and will push to shift blame onto you.
  • Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations (49 C.F.R.) apply to every interstate trucking company using US-17 — hours-of-service violations, inadequate maintenance, and unqualified drivers all create direct liability for the carrier.
  • The Port of Brunswick was the nation's busiest port for automobiles in 2025 for the second straight year, handling 779,000 autos (Georgia Ports Authority) — but its Colonel's Island terminal sits south of Brunswick with direct I-95 access, not on the Broadfield stretch.
  • Glynn County tort lawsuits typically file in Glynn County State Court in Brunswick — though Glynn County Superior Court (Brunswick Judicial Circuit) has concurrent jurisdiction, and diversity cases may go to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Georgia, Brunswick Division.
  • Georgia UM/UIM coverage under O.C.G.A. § 33-7-11 is critical when the at-fault trucker is uninsured, underinsured, or absconds — Georgia lets you stack UM coverage across multiple policies, and whether you elected "added-on" or "reduced-by" coverage decides how much of it survives the trucker's liability limits.
  • A wrongful-death claim under O.C.G.A. § 51-4-1 et seq. belongs to the spouse, children, or parents — separate from the estate's survival claim — and the 2-year clock applies the same way.

Why US-17 / GA-25 Through Broadfield Is a Truck-Crash Hot Spot

US-17 and GA-25 share the same alignment through Glynn County under the name "Ocean Highway." North of Chapel Crossing Road it is a two-lane U.S. highway that parallels I-95 a few miles to the west — but it carries a very different traffic mix. Local commuters drive between Brunswick and Darien. Beach-bound tourists use it as a slower, scenic alternative to the interstate. That mix of commuters, tourists, and local delivery trucks is the problem. Outside urban and residential districts, Georgia's statutory limit on a highway like this is 55 mph (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-181), lower only where posted. A loaded tractor-trailer at 55 mph closing on a passenger car slowing for a left turn at Grants Ferry Road (SR-99) creates a closing-speed differential that 2-lane geometry was never designed to absorb. The two-lane section has no median and no continuous center turn lane, and driveway curb-cuts are frequent. GDOT is widening US-17 from Yacht Road to SR 99 to four lanes with a raised median and turn lanes, plus a roundabout at the SR 99 intersection; Phase 1 was expected to open in 2025 and Phase 2 in 2026, and lane closures with flagging were still in place in July 2026. According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), 5,375 large trucks were involved in fatal crashes nationwide in 2023 (down from 5,873 in 2022), 5,472 people died in those crashes, and 70% of the dead were occupants of the other vehicle, not the truck. That ratio tracks what we see on US-17: when an 80,000-pound rig collides with a 4,000-pound sedan, the physics decide the outcome before the lawyers ever get involved. According to the Georgia Governor's Office of Highway Safety (GOHS), Georgia recorded 1,615 traffic fatalities in 2023, down 10% from 1,796 in 2022; one-third of the state's fatal crashes happen in rural areas that hold only 21% of its population, 45% involve a vehicle leaving its lane or the road, and 12% involve a large truck.

What to Do in the First 72 Hours After a US-17 Truck Crash

The first three days set the ceiling on your recovery. Trucking companies dispatch rapid-response teams to crash scenes within hours — investigators, photographers, and defense counsel whose job is to lock in a narrative that protects the carrier. You should not try to match that infrastructure alone.
  1. Get medical care immediately — even if you feel "fine." Adrenaline masks traumatic brain and spinal-cord injuries. Southeast Georgia Health System – Brunswick Campus is the county hospital; Memorial Health University Medical Center in Savannah is the region's only Level I trauma center. Document every visit. If you are dealing with a possible head injury, our Darien brain injury lawyers and Darien spinal cord injury lawyers can help you understand what to watch for in the days after impact.
  2. Photograph everything — the scene, both vehicles, the truck's USDOT number and trailer markings, skid marks, and your visible injuries.
  3. Request the police report from the Georgia State Patrol (Post 23, Brunswick) or the Glynn County Police Department, whose Records Section releases crash reports, and get the responding officer's name.
  4. Do not give a recorded statement to the trucking company's insurer. Not the next day. Not the next week. Not before you talk to a lawyer.
  5. Send a preservation-of-evidence letter to the carrier — through counsel — to preserve the truck's ECM (engine control module) data, hours-of-service logs, dashcam footage, dispatch records, and maintenance files. Federal rules require carriers to keep hours-of-service records for only six months (49 C.F.R. § 395.8(k)) and inspection reports for three (49 C.F.R. § 396.11); ECM and dashcam data have no federal retention rule.

How Georgia Law Shapes Your US-17 Truck Claim

A US-17 truck crash is a Georgia case. Three statutes drive almost every conversation about value. Statute of limitations — O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33. You have two years from the date of injury to file a personal-injury lawsuit. Miss that window and your case is over. If the crash involves a city vehicle (Brunswick, Darien) you must serve ante-litem notice within 6 months under O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5. If the crash involves a Glynn County vehicle, claims against the county must be presented within 12 months under O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1. If the crash involves a state vehicle or the State of Georgia, you must serve ante-litem notice within 12 months under O.C.G.A. § 50-21-26. Missed notice is fatal. Comparative negligence — O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33. Georgia uses a 50% bar. If a jury finds you 50% or more at fault, you take nothing. If you are 30% at fault, your damages are reduced by 30%. Trucking-defense lawyers work this rule hard — they will argue you were speeding, distracted, or in the truck's no-zone. A practiced Georgia truck accident lawyer reconstructs the crash with EDR data and human-factors testimony to push fault back where it belongs. Wrongful death — O.C.G.A. § 51-4-1 et seq. If a US-17 truck crash takes a life, Georgia law gives the surviving spouse (and then children, and then parents) the right to file a wrongful-death claim for the "full value of the life" of the deceased — and the estate may pursue a separate survival action. Our Darien wrongful death lawyers handle these claims for Glynn and McIntosh County families. Eric Roden, the founding partner of Roden Law, has spent more than a decade litigating catastrophic trucking cases on the Georgia coast and consistently makes the same point: in a US-17 corridor case, the carrier's records win or lose the case before a jury ever hears opening statements. Hours-of-service logs, ECM data, and dispatch communications routinely reveal violations the police report never captures — and Georgia's 2-year statute of limitations, combined with the carrier's right to discard logs after six months and overwrite ECM and dashcam data at any time, means a family that waits is a family that loses leverage. The first weeks are not paperwork. They are the case.

When DUI and Uninsured Motorists Enter the Picture

O.C.G.A. § 40-6-391 governs Georgia DUI, and a criminal conviction is persuasive evidence in a parallel civil claim. If the at-fault driver was knowingly served while noticeably intoxicated by a Glynn County bar or restaurant that knew he would soon be driving, O.C.G.A. § 51-1-40 (Georgia's dram-shop statute) may open a claim against the establishment — a deeper pocket than a single uninsured driver. When the at-fault trucker or driver is uninsured or underinsured, O.C.G.A. § 33-7-11 governs your UM/UIM recovery. Georgia lets you stack UM coverage from more than one policy (Dairyland Ins. Co. v. State Farm, Ga. Ct. App. 2008), and the written election you made between "added-on" and "reduced-by" coverage under O.C.G.A. § 33-7-11(b)(1)(D)(ii) decides whether your UM limits sit on top of the trucker's policy or are reduced by it. Where US-17 Cases File in Glynn County

Most Glynn County car-and-truck tort lawsuits are filed in Glynn County State Court in Brunswick, which hears civil actions without regard to the amount in controversy (O.C.G.A. § 15-7-4). Glynn County Superior Court, the Brunswick Judicial Circuit's court of general jurisdiction, can hear the same suits and has exclusive jurisdiction over felonies, divorce, equity, and title to land. Glynn County Magistrate Court handles civil claims of $15,000 or less. Cases with diversity of citizenship — for example, a Florida-based motor carrier sued by a Georgia plaintiff for more than $75,000 — often end up in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Georgia, Brunswick Division.
