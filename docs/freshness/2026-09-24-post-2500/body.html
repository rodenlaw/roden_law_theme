Are Police Reports Required in Georgia?

Georgia law requires the driver to notify law enforcement of a crash immediately in the following circumstances (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-273):
  • An injury or fatality resulted from the crash
  • There was apparent property damage of $500 or more
Separately, your auto policy may require you to report the crash to your insurer, and many insurers ask for the police report when you make a claim. That is a policy condition, not a legal reporting duty.

What Errors Could I Find On My Police Report?

There are generally three types of errors that you might find on your police report, including:

Factual Mistakes

A factual mistake is easier to make than you may realize. Crash scenes are often chaotic. The first responders may initially be more focused on getting medical help for anyone who is injured. Maybe the officer is at the end of his or her shift or had a bad night. Like all of us, they are vulnerable to making mistakes. A factual mistake could include:
  • Wrong make, model or color of your vehicle or the other driver’s vehicle
  • License plate numbers may be wrong
  • Incorrect driver’s license numbers
  • Incorrect names and addresses of either driver involved
  • Wrong time and/or date for when the crash occurred
  • Location is not where the crash occurred

Facts From Your Statement Are Incorrect or Left Out

The responding officer may have written your statement down wrong. For instance, say you said the other driver ran a red light, but the officer wrote that you said the other driver had a yellow light. Omission errors are also common. For example, if you told the officer that you injured your back and neck in the crash, but he or she instead documented that you said you had no injuries.

Disputed Errors

Disputed errors are the officer's conclusions, such as who was at fault or what contributed to the crash. These are the hardest to change, because only the investigating officer and agency can amend the report, and they will not rewrite a conclusion simply because a driver disagrees with it. These types of errors, especially, may be best handled with the assistance of a lawyer.

Why It Is Important To Remove Errors From Your Police Report

Mistakes in a police report could negatively affect your claim in many ways. Whether the information is wrong, left out or there are some other errors, it is important to seek a correction as quickly as possible. For instance, if you do not correct the fact about the other driver running the red light, you could get unfairly blamed for more than your share of fault. If that happens, it could impact how much you get compensated for your losses. This is because Georgia follows a modified comparative fault system (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33).

Georgia’s Modified Comparative Negligence Rule

If you are deemed to be 50 percent or more at fault for an accident in Georgia, you cannot recover any of your damages. Even if you are assessed with less than 50 percent of the fault, any compensation you receive will be reduced. An example of this is if you are assessed with 20 percent of the fault when you were actually not at fault at all. If you were awarded $100,000 for your damages in that situation, you would only get $80,000. That is $20,000 lost on an error.

How Do I Get Mistakes Removed

Depending on the type of error you find, there are several ways to seek a correction to your police report.

Get a Copy of Your Georgia Crash Report

The first step is to request a copy of your crash report right away. Statewide, the Georgia Department of Transportation (GDOT) is the custodian of Georgia crash reports; you can buy a copy through BuyCrash or mail GDOT's Individual Crash Report Request form ($5 per report, $2 more for a certified copy). Anyone named in the report, such as a driver, an injured person or their attorney, is entitled to it under O.C.G.A. § 50-18-72(a)(5). If Savannah Police investigated, you can also request a copy of your police report from its Records Unit in person, by phone, by fax, snail mail or by email; it may take up to three business days. The longer you wait to fix a report with errors on it, the more likely it is the officer who documented your report may not remember details about it.

Correcting Factual Mistakes

For clerical errors, contacting the investigating officer with a document that shows the correct detail (your registration for a wrong plate number, your license for a misspelled name) is usually all it takes; the officer can then issue a corrected or supplemental report.

Changing or Amending Subjective Information:

Statements that are subjective or disputable are tougher to fix, but you can try to get these changed or at least amended by taking these steps:
  • Contact the officer listed on the report and politely explain the error.
  • Ask if they can correct the statement.
  • If they will not change the statement, write your own account of the crash (concise, factual, signed and dated) and give it to your attorney. Whether the agency will attach a driver's statement to the official report is at its discretion; what your statement reliably does is put your version in front of the insurance adjuster and, if needed, the court.
  • When you write your statement, be sure that it is not just your opinion. Provide the facts without rambling – be clear, be brief and give details. If you have additional supporting evidence, like photos or dash cam footage, work with your attorney on the best way to send this statement.
