Savannah is one of Georgia's most beautiful cities — but its roads tell a different story. With a mix of high-speed suburban corridors, aging infrastructure, and heavy traffic volumes, certain Savannah intersections see far more crashes than others. According to the Governor's Office of Highway Safety, Chatham County recorded 39 traffic deaths in 2023, the sixth-most of any Georgia county, and a 2018 WTOC analysis of Savannah crash reports found almost half of the serious intersection crashes happened between noon and 6 p.m. If you or a loved one has been injured at one of these dangerous intersections, understanding your legal rights under Georgia law — and the legal concept of negligence — is the first step toward obtaining fair compensation.

Why Savannah's Intersections Are So Dangerous

Several factors combine to make Savannah's intersections hazardous. Growth along the Abercorn Street corridor has pushed heavy traffic onto roads such as Abercorn and DeRenne Avenue. Many Savannah intersections lack protected left-turn signals, dedicated turn lanes, or modern traffic control systems. The result is a dangerous mix of high-speed traffic, limited visibility, and driver confusion that produces hundreds of crashes each year.

Tourism adds another layer of risk. Savannah drew about 12.9 million visitors in 2024, according to the City of Savannah, many of whom are unfamiliar with local roads. Visitors unfamiliar with the downtown one-way grid and the Southside's multi-lane commercial corridors add to the risk.

Additionally, Savannah's flat coastal terrain and frequent afternoon rainstorms create dangerous conditions. Standing water at intersections reduces tire traction and visibility, and hydroplaning can contribute to rear-end and broadside crashes at Savannah's busiest crossroads.

The Highest-Crash Intersections in Savannah

This list draws on the 2023 Georgia DOT crash data reviewed by Bike Walk Savannah (Savannah Morning News, Jan. 2025), WTOC's analysis of 2017 Savannah crash reports, and Chatham County's 2025 Safe Streets for All safety plan. In 2023, Savannah's 10 highest-crash intersections accounted for 428 of the city's 7,975 crashes, led by the I-16 interchanges and the Abercorn Street corridor. The numbering is not a ranking.

1. Abercorn Street and White Bluff Road

This intersection was among Savannah's 10 highest-crash intersections in 2023, and it is one of four Savannah intersections with a red-light camera. The convergence of heavy commercial traffic from Oglethorpe Mall, residential traffic from the White Bluff corridor, and vehicles entering and exiting nearby shopping centers creates constant conflict points. Rear-end collisions and T-bone crashes are common here. If you have been hurt in a crash at this intersection, a Savannah car accident lawyer can help you investigate whether the at-fault driver ran a red light, was distracted, or failed to yield.

2. Interstate 16 and Chatham Parkway

The I-16/Chatham Parkway interchange was among Savannah's highest-crash locations in 2023. I-16 carried about 59,600 vehicles a day between Chatham Parkway and Dean Forest Road in GDOT's 2019 count. Merging at highway speed and sudden braking at the interchange create real risk; GDOT's 16@95 projects added ramp meters on the Chatham Parkway on-ramps and a third lane on I-16 in each direction between I-95 and Chatham Parkway. Truck traffic is heavy along this corridor, as I-16 is one of Georgia's busiest freight corridors, carrying Port of Savannah traffic west toward Macon and to I-95. Truck accidents at this interchange frequently involve catastrophic injuries due to the speed differential between commercial vehicles and passenger cars.

3. King George Boulevard and Abercorn Street

King George Boulevard now meets the Abercorn Street Extension at a grade-separated interchange that GDOT opened in December 2017 at a cost of $28.5 million, replacing the old signalized intersection. Pedestrians crossing Abercorn between King George Boulevard and Veterans Parkway are still at elevated risk; Chatham County's safety plan identifies that stretch as a cluster of pedestrian deaths and serious injuries.

4. Abercorn Street and Rio Road

The Abercorn/Rio Road intersection, located in a busy commercial area, was among Savannah's highest-crash intersections in WTOC's analysis of 2017 crash reports, with crashes driven by constant traffic entering and exiting retail parking lots, the proximity of multiple signalized and unsignalized access points, and aggressive driving by motorists frustrated with congestion. Distracted driving — particularly drivers looking at storefronts or phone navigation — is the biggest crash trend Savannah's traffic engineers see citywide.

5. DeRenne Avenue and Truman Parkway

DeRenne Avenue serves as Savannah's primary east-west artery through Midtown, and its interchange with the Truman Parkway handles enormous traffic volumes as commuters travel between the residential Eastside and the commercial Southside. The narrow lanes on DeRenne, combined with frequent sudden stops and aggressive lane changes, make this area a hotspot for rear-end collisions. The proximity of Memorial Health University Medical Center increases pedestrian traffic in this area, creating additional hazards. Nearby, Abercorn Street at East DeRenne Avenue was among the 2023 highest-crash intersections and has a red-light camera. Victims of crashes here may suffer traumatic brain injuries or spinal cord injuries that require extensive medical treatment.

6. Ogeechee Road and Chatham Parkway

This West Savannah intersection becomes especially hazardous during peak commuting hours. Ogeechee Road carries significant industrial and commercial traffic, including heavy trucks serving the port and warehouse districts. Chatham County's 2025 safety plan lists Ogeechee Road at Chatham Parkway among its fatal-and-serious-injury intersections, with about 42,000 vehicles entering a day. Heavy turning volumes increase the risk of broadside collisions. Motorcycle riders are particularly vulnerable at this intersection because other drivers frequently fail to check for smaller vehicles before turning.

7. Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard and 37th Street

Located near the border between Savannah's historic district and Midtown, this intersection sees a mix of local residential traffic, tourist traffic, and commercial vehicles. A $9.9 million federal Safe Streets for All grant funds safety upgrades at 15 intersections along 37th Street, including updated signals and protected left turns. The relatively narrow lanes, on-street parking, and pedestrian activity in this area create conditions where distracted or speeding drivers are especially dangerous. Pedestrian accidents are a serious concern at this intersection, particularly during evening hours when visibility is reduced.

8. Abercorn Street and Victory Drive (US-80)

The meeting of Abercorn Street and Victory Drive creates a complex intersection. Victory Drive carries heavy traffic between Savannah's islands and the city's interior, and Abercorn brings heavy north-south commercial traffic. The multiple turn lanes and signal phases at this intersection can confuse unfamiliar drivers. When an intersection crash is fatal, the family may have wrongful death claims.

Savannah's Highest-Crash Road Corridors

Beyond individual intersections, several Savannah road corridors stand out for their overall crash frequency.

Abercorn Street (Full Corridor)

Abercorn Street is one of Savannah's highest-crash roads. Stretching from the Historic District through Midtown and deep into the Southside suburbs, Abercorn handles enormous traffic volumes and is lined with commercial development that generates constant turning movements and driveway conflicts. Three of Savannah's 10 highest-crash intersections in 2023 were on Abercorn, and all four of the city's red-light-camera intersections are on Abercorn, according to GDOT's 2024 report.

DeRenne Avenue

DeRenne Avenue is notorious for its narrow lanes, heavy congestion, and aggressive driving. As the primary east-west route through Midtown Savannah, DeRenne carries heavy commuter traffic and is often congested. Sudden stops, aggressive lane changes, and the clustering of medical facilities near Memorial Health create a constant stream of hazards for drivers, cyclists, and pedestrians.

Interstate 16

I-16 between Savannah and the I-95 interchange carries heavy freight and commuter traffic. Heavy merging volumes contribute to rear-end and sideswipe collisions. Truck crashes on I-16 are often catastrophic: nationally, 70 percent of people killed in large-truck crashes are in the other vehicle, according to NHTSA's 2024 data. If a truck driver's negligence caused your crash on I-16, you may have claims against both the driver and the trucking company.

Common Causes of Intersection Crashes in Savannah

Understanding what causes intersection crashes helps accident victims identify the at-fault party and build a strong injury claim. The most common causes of intersection crashes in Savannah include:

  • Red light running: Drivers who enter an intersection after the light has turned red cause some of the most violent T-bone collisions. Georgia law under O.C.G.A. § 40-6-20 requires drivers to obey all traffic control devices.
  • Failure to yield on left turns: Left-turn accidents are among the most common intersection crash types in Savannah. Drivers who misjudge the speed of oncoming traffic or attempt to turn during a yellow light frequently cause broadside collisions.
  • Distracted driving: Cell phone use, GPS navigation, and passenger distractions cause drivers to miss red lights, stop signs, and the movements of other vehicles in intersections.
  • Speeding: Excessive speed reduces a driver's ability to stop at intersections and dramatically increases the severity of injuries in a crash. Georgia's speed limits are governed by O.C.G.A. § 40-6-181.
  • Impaired driving: Drunk and drugged driving is especially dangerous during late-night and early-morning hours. According to the Governor's Office of Highway Safety, 27% of Georgia traffic deaths in 2023 were alcohol-related.
  • Poor road design: Some Savannah intersections lack adequate turn lanes, sight lines, signage, or signal timing. When a government entity's failure to maintain or properly design a road contributes to a crash, the injured party may have a claim against the responsible agency under Georgia's governmental immunity exceptions. Those claims require written notice first: within 6 months for a city (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5) and 12 months for a county (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1) or the State (O.C.G.A. § 50-21-26). Counties are largely immune from these suits (O.C.G.A. § 36-1-4).

Georgia Traffic Laws That Protect You

Georgia has specific traffic laws that govern driver behavior at intersections and provide a legal framework for holding negligent drivers accountable after a crash.

Statute of limitations: Under O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33, you have two years from the date of your accident to file a personal injury lawsuit in Georgia. Missing this deadline typically means losing your right to compensation entirely.

Comparative fault: Georgia follows a modified comparative fault rule under O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33. You can recover compensation as long as you are less than 50% at fault for the accident, but your award is reduced by your percentage of fault. For example, if you suffered $100,000 in damages but were found 20% at fault, your recovery would be $80,000.

Right-of-way rules: O.C.G.A. §§ 40-6-70 through 40-6-73 set right-of-way rules at intersections, including yielding when turning left and at stop and yield signs. O.C.G.A. § 40-6-20 requires obeying traffic signals, and O.C.G.A. § 40-6-91 requires drivers to stop and remain stopped for pedestrians in crosswalks. Violations of these statutes can serve as strong evidence of negligence in your injury claim.

Hit and run: Under O.C.G.A. § 40-6-270, leaving the scene of an accident that causes a death or serious injury is a felony in Georgia; leaving after a crash with lesser injuries is a misdemeanor. If a hit-and-run driver injured you at a Savannah intersection, your car accident attorney can work with law enforcement and use surveillance footage, witness statements, and other evidence to identify the responsible driver.

What to Do After an Intersection Accident in Savannah

The steps you take immediately after an intersection crash in Savannah can significantly affect your ability to recover full compensation. Here is what you should do:

  1. Call 911 and request medical attention. Georgia law requires you to report any accident that causes injury, death, or apparent property damage of $500 or more (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-273). The police report generated from this call becomes critical evidence in your claim.
  2. Seek medical treatment immediately. Even if you feel fine at the scene, many serious injuries — including traumatic brain injuries and soft tissue injuries — may not produce symptoms until hours or days later. Prompt medical documentation creates a direct link between the crash and your injuries.
  3. Document the scene. Take photographs of vehicle damage, the intersection layout, traffic signals, road conditions, skid marks, and any visible injuries. If there are surveillance cameras nearby — which is common at major Savannah intersections — note their locations.
  4. Exchange information with all involved drivers, including names, insurance information, license plate numbers, and contact details. Get names and phone numbers from any witnesses.
  5. Do not admit fault or apologize at the scene. Even a simple "I'm sorry" can be used against you to argue comparative fault under Georgia law.
  6. Contact a Savannah personal injury lawyer before giving any recorded statements to insurance companies. Insurance adjusters are trained to minimize your claim, and early legal representation protects your rights.

Proving Fault at a Dangerous Intersection

Establishing liability after an intersection crash often requires more than just your account of what happened. An experienced attorney will gather and analyze:

  • Traffic camera and surveillance footage — four Savannah intersections, all on Abercorn Street, have red-light cameras, and many more have nearby business security cameras that capture the moments before and during a crash.
  • The official police accident report — including any citations issued to the at-fault driver, the officer's determination of fault, and witness statements recorded at the scene.
  • Accident reconstruction evidence — in serious crashes, expert accident reconstructionists can analyze vehicle damage patterns, skid marks, and impact angles to determine exactly how the collision occurred.
  • Cell phone records — if distracted driving is suspected, your attorney can subpoena the at-fault driver's phone records to prove they were texting, calling, or using an app at the time of the crash.
  • Road design and maintenance records — if a dangerous road condition contributed to the crash, records from the Georgia Department of Transportation or the City of Savannah may reveal a known hazard that was not addressed.
