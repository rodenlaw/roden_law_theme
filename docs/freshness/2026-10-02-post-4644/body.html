On March 2, 2026, a motorcyclist was killed on Dorchester Road at the intersection with Forest Hills Drive — in a collision with a heavy-duty pickup truck. Police said no charges were expected. SCDOT's road safety audit counted 17 fatal crashes and 924 injury crashes on Dorchester Road between the Dorchester County line and Kent Avenue from 2018 through 2022.

Dorchester Road's combination of high speeds, heavy truck traffic, and unprotected left-turn intersections creates a recipe for deadly motorcycle collisions. Here's what the data and evidence tell us.

Why Dorchester Road Kills Motorcyclists

The Left-Turn Problem

Nationally, in 45% of fatal crashes between a motorcycle and one other vehicle, the other vehicle was turning left (NHTSA, 2024 data). On Dorchester Road, this statistic comes to life at every unprotected left-turn intersection:

  • A car waits to turn left
  • The driver scans oncoming traffic but fails to register an approaching motorcycle (smaller visual profile = perceived as farther away)
  • The driver turns directly into the motorcycle's path
  • At 50 mph, a motorcycle covers about 73 feet every second

In Dorchester County, the Dorchester Road intersections with Old Trolley Road, Ladson Road and Bacons Bridge Road were three of the county's five highest-crash intersections in 2024 (SCDPS).

Heavy Truck Traffic

Cement mixers, dump trucks, and construction vehicles share Dorchester Road with motorcycles. These vehicles create specific hazards:

  • Massive blind spots: A motorcycle is invisible in a truck's blind zone
  • Air turbulence: Wind wash from large trucks can destabilize a motorcycle
  • Debris: Gravel, concrete, and construction material falling from trucks
  • Obscured sight lines: Trucks in turning lanes block left-turning drivers' view of approaching motorcycles

Speed

Higher speeds make crashes far deadlier for riders.

The Helmet Question

South Carolina law requires motorcycle operators and passengers under age 21 to wear an approved helmet (S.C. Code § 56-5-3660). State law does not impose that helmet requirement on riders age 21 or older. For riders 21 and older, the South Carolina Supreme Court has held that riding without a helmet is not contributory negligence or assumption of risk (Mayes v. Paxton, 313 S.C. 109 (1993)).

What Families Should Know

If your loved one was killed on Dorchester Road, South Carolina's wrongful death law lets the personal representative of the estate recover, for the family (S.C. Code §§ 15-51-20, 15-51-40):

  • Funeral and burial expenses
  • Lost financial support the deceased would have provided
  • Loss of companionship, guidance, and consortium
  • Pain and suffering the victim experienced before death, through a separate survival action brought by the estate (S.C. Code § 15-5-90)
  • Punitive damages if the at-fault driver acted recklessly

What Needs to Change

  • Protected left-turn signals at Bacons Bridge Road and other high-crash intersections
  • Motorcycle awareness signage in high-conflict zones
  • Speed enforcement on the corridor
  • Truck route restrictions during peak motorcycle riding hours
