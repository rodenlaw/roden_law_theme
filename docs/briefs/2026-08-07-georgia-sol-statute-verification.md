## The SB 68 question — resolved

**SB 68 does not amend O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33.** This matters because secondary reporting
loosely says SB 68 "clarified and reinforced" the 50% bar, which reads as though the
statute changed. It did not.

All three references to § 51-12-33 in the bill are **cross-references** from the new
negligent-security provisions (§ 51-3-56), instructing the trier of fact to apportion
fault *under* § 51-12-33. The apportionment statute itself is untouched, so the 2024
text — including the 50% bar at (g) — is current.

What SB 68 actually did (from the bill's own section headers):

| § | Amends / adds | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | revises § 9-10-184 | arguing the monetary value of noneconomic damages (anchoring) |
| 2 | § 9-11-12 | answer, defenses, objections — timing |
| 3 | § 9-11-41 | dismissal of actions, recommencement |
| 4 | new section, Title 9 Ch. 15 | attorney's fees, court costs, litigation expenses |
| 5 | § 40-8-76.1 | **seat-belt evidence now admissible** |
| 6 | Title 51 Ch. 3 — new §§ | negligent security cause of action |
| 7 | new section, Title 51 Ch. 12 Art. 1 | special damages for medical/healthcare expenses |
| 8 | new § 51-12-15 | bifurcation (carve-out under $150,000 in controversy) |

**Effective date and applicability, verbatim from Section 9:**

> "(a) This Act shall become effective upon its approval by the Governor... (b) Sections 6
> and 7 of this Act shall apply only with respect to causes of action arising on or after
> the effective date of this Act, and any prior causes of action shall be governed by prior
> law. It is the intention of the General Assembly that all other provisions of this Act
> shall apply to causes of action pending on the effective date of this Act, unless such
> application would be unconstitutional."

So: negligent security and phantom damages are **prospective only**; everything else
reaches **pending** cases. Approved 2025-04-21.

## Verified deadlines

### O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33 — personal injury
> "actions for injuries to the person shall be brought within **two years** after the right
> of action accrues, except for injuries to the reputation, which shall be brought within
> **one year**... and except for actions for injuries to the person involving **loss of
> consortium**, which shall be brought within **four years**."

Three deadlines in one sentence. The consortium and defamation carve-outs are in the
statute itself — most competitor pages cite only the two years.

### O.C.G.A. § 9-3-30 — damage to real property
> "All actions for trespass upon or damage to realty shall be brought within **four years**
> after the right of action accrues."

### O.C.G.A. § 9-3-32 — personal property
> "Actions for the recovery of personal property, or for damages for the conversion or
> destruction of the same, shall be brought within **four years** after the right of action
> accrues."

This is the correct cite for a totalled vehicle — not § 9-3-30, which is realty.

### O.C.G.A. § 9-3-71 — medical malpractice
> "(a) ...an action for medical malpractice shall be brought within **two years** after the
> date on which an injury or death arising from a negligent or wrongful act or omission
> occurred. (b) ...in no event may an action for medical malpractice be brought more than
> **five years** after the date on which the negligent or wrongful act or omission occurred.
> (c) Subsection (a)... is intended to create a two-year statute of limitations. Subsection
> (b)... is intended to create a five-year statute of ultimate repose and abrogation."

### O.C.G.A. § 9-3-73 — med-mal disabilities
Minors **five and older** and legally incompetent persons get the ordinary med-mal periods.
A minor **under five** has "two years from the date of such minor's fifth birthday."
Hard outer limits: no action after the **tenth birthday** for a child under five at the time
of the act; **five years** for a child five or older; **five years** for a legally
incompetent person.

### O.C.G.A. § 34-9-82 — workers' compensation ⚠ highest-value cell
> "(a) The right to compensation shall be barred unless a claim therefor is filed within
> **one year after injury**, except that if payment of weekly benefits has been made or
> remedial treatment has been furnished by the employer on account of the injury the claim
> may be filed within **one year after the date of the last remedial treatment** furnished by
> the employer or within **two years after the date of the last payment of weekly benefits**.
> (b) The right to compensation for death shall be barred unless a claim therefor is filed
> within **one year after the death** of the employee."

Two things competitors get wrong: the tort SOL is *not* the comp deadline, and the one-year
clock **resets** off employer-furnished treatment or benefit payments. This exact confusion
already produced a live error on this site — 13 pages told Georgia workers they had 2 years.

### O.C.G.A. § 34-9-80 — workers' comp notice
Notice must be given immediately or as soon as practicable. "No compensation will be payable
unless such notice, either oral or written, is given within **30 days** after the occurrence
of an accident or within 30 days after death" — subject to excuses for incapacity, fraud,
employer knowledge, or reasonable excuse without prejudice.

### O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5 — claims against a **city** ⚠ second-highest-value cell
*(Justia serves the 2022 edition for this section; History shows last amended Ga. L. 2014.)*

> "Within **six months** of the happening of the event upon which a claim against a municipal
> corporation is predicated, the person... shall present the claim in writing to the governing
> authority... stating the time, place, and extent of the injury, as nearly as practicable,
> and the negligence which caused the injury."

Also verified, and largely absent from the competing pages:
- The notice **must state the specific amount of monetary damages sought**; that amount is an
  offer of compromise and is **not binding** on the claimant later
- The governing authority has **30 days** to act
- The statute of limitations is **suspended** while the demand is pending
- Service must be on the **mayor or the chairperson of the city council or commission**,
  personally or by certified mail or statutory overnight delivery

### O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 — claims against a **county**
> "All claims against counties must be presented within **12 months** after they accrue or
> become payable or the same are barred, provided that minors or other persons laboring under
> disabilities shall be allowed 12 months after the removal of the disability."

### O.C.G.A. § 50-21-26 — claims against the **state** (GTCA)
> "Notice of a claim shall be given in writing within **12 months** of the date the loss was
> discovered or should have been discovered."

Also verified: notice goes by certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, return receipt
requested, to the **Risk Management Division of the Department of Administrative Services**,
with a copy to the state entity; the complaint must attach the notice and receipt as exhibits
or be dismissed without prejudice if not cured within 30 days.

### Tolling

**§ 9-3-90** — legally incompetent persons get the same period after the disability is
removed; individuals "less than 18 years of age when a cause of action accrues shall be
entitled to the same time after he or she reaches the age of 18 years," except as provided in
§ 9-3-33.1.

**§ 9-3-92** — the time between death and the commencement of estate representation "shall not
be counted against his estate," capped at **five years**.

**§ 9-3-99** — tort limitations are tolled while a criminal prosecution arising from the same
facts is pending, "provided that such time does not exceed **six years**."

### O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33 — comparative fault (confirmed current)
> "(g) ...the plaintiff shall not be entitled to receive any damages if the plaintiff is
> **50 percent or more responsible** for the injury or damages claimed."

Supporting subsections: (a) damages reduced in proportion to the plaintiff's own fault;
(b) apportionment among liable parties is **several, not joint**, with no right of
contribution; (c) the trier of fact considers the fault of all persons or entities including
**nonparties**, with (c)(1) requiring either a settlement with the nonparty or notice not
later than **120 days** before trial.

## Not verified — do not state without checking

- **Wrongful death — 2 years.** Georgia has no dedicated wrongful-death limitations statute;
  the period derives from § 9-3-33 plus case law, and § 9-3-92 can toll it up to five years
  while an estate is unrepresented. Write it carefully, or cite § 9-3-33 and § 9-3-92 and
  describe the mechanism rather than asserting a bare "2 years."
- **§ 9-3-33.1** — referenced as an exception inside both § 9-3-90 and § 9-3-99 (childhood
  sexual abuse). Not retrieved. Do not characterize it.
- **The discovery rule.** Georgia's is narrow and largely judge-made for continuing torts and
  latent injury. No statute was verified for it. Either omit, or state only what § 9-3-71(a)
  and § 50-21-26 say on their own terms.
- **Punitive damages cap / § 51-12-5.1(f).** The truck pillar's own meta asserts the $250,000
  cap is removed in DUI cases. Plausible but unverified here.
