## 3. The angle

The problem is real: essentially all SB 68 content is written from the defense and business
side, in the register of "Georgia sheds its judicial-hellhole reputation." Roden Law is
plaintiff-side. Two bad responses are available and both must be avoided:

- **The mirror-image error** — writing an advocacy piece that overstates the damage, implies
  injured people have lost rights they still have, and frightens readers into calling. This
  is both dishonest and legally risky. It is also, notably, where the ranking plaintiff pages
  are weakest.
- **The neutral-summary error** — writing the sixteenth "key changes" explainer. Fifteen
  firms with more authority already did it, and it serves nobody who is actually hurt.

### The angle that is both honest and unoccupied

**Answer the question the reader actually has — "does this hurt my case?" — with the
applicability rules, which almost nobody writing for a general audience gets right.**

The verified record contains two facts that are directly, personally actionable for an
injured reader, that are reassuring rather than alarming, that are unambiguously true, and
that are absent from the competitor coverage reviewed for this brief:

**(a) Two of the eight sections do not apply to older claims at all.** Section 9(b) of the
bill: Sections 6 (negligent security) and 7 (medical special damages) "shall apply only with
respect to causes of action arising on or after the effective date of this Act, and any prior
causes of action shall be governed by prior law." A reader whose injury predates 2025-04-21
is untouched by the two sections most often described as the harshest.

**(b) The bifurcation section carries a carve-out under $150,000 in controversy.** Most
ordinary injury claims are below that line. Competitor coverage describes bifurcation as
something that could "double the length and complexity of a case" without mentioning the
carve-out at all.

Pair those with the correction that leads §4 below — SB 68 **did not** change the 50% bar or
the filing deadlines — and the page has a clear, defensible, genuinely useful thesis:

> Some of this is real and one change matters to ordinary car-crash clients. Most of it is
> procedural and is your lawyer's problem, not yours. And several of the things people have
> been told changed did not change.

That is not spin in either direction. It is what the bill says. It is also the only version
of this page that a Georgia-admitted attorney can put a byline on without qualification.

### Tone rules

- Do not use "judicial hellhole," and do not rebut it. Do not litigate the politics of the
  bill. Do not characterize the legislature's motives, the insurance industry's motives, or
  who "won."
- Do not tell a reader their case is now harder. Tell them which sections could apply to a
  claim like theirs and which cannot, and that the answer depends on the date their cause of
  action arose.
- No prediction. Nothing about how courts will interpret it, whether any part will be
  challenged, or what it will do to settlement values.
- One change genuinely deserves a plain warning to ordinary clients: **seat-belt evidence is
  now admissible** under Section 5. State the fact. Do not characterize its effect (see §4).

## 4. Accuracy — the controlling constraint on this page

SB 68 is recent, complex, and widely mis-summarized, and this is YMYL legal content. The
accuracy rules here are stricter than on any other page in the library.

### The rule

**The writer may assert about SB 68 only what appears in
`docs/briefs/2026-08-07-georgia-sol-statute-verification.md`.** That record was read against
the signed 19-page bill on 2026-08-07. Anything not in it must be verified against the signed
text or omitted. **Do not fill a gap from memory, from a competitor page, from a law-firm
client alert, or from a news summary.** Every secondary source on this topic that was
reviewed for this brief got at least one thing wrong.

### Allowlist — may be stated as written

- SB 68 was approved by the Governor on **2025-04-21** and became effective upon approval.
- It has eight substantive sections. **These are the subjects, and the subjects are all that
  is verified:**

| § | Amends / adds | Subject — state it at this level of detail and no further |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | revises § 9-10-184 | arguing the monetary value of noneconomic damages (anchoring) |
| 2 | § 9-11-12 | answer, defenses, objections — timing |
| 3 | § 9-11-41 | dismissal of actions, recommencement |
| 4 | new section, Title 9 Ch. 15 | attorney's fees, court costs, litigation expenses |
| 5 | § 40-8-76.1 | **seat-belt evidence now admissible** |
| 6 | new §§ in Title 51 Ch. 3 | negligent security cause of action |
| 7 | new section, Title 51 Ch. 12 Art. 1 | special damages for medical/healthcare expenses |
| 8 | new § 51-12-15 | bifurcation, with a carve-out under **$150,000** in controversy |

- **Applicability (Section 9, verbatim in the record):** Sections 6 and 7 apply only to causes
  of action arising on or after the effective date; prior causes of action are governed by
  prior law. All other provisions are intended to apply to causes of action **pending** on the
  effective date, unless that application would be unconstitutional.
- **SB 68 does not amend O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33.** All three references to § 51-12-33 in the bill
  are cross-references from the new negligent-security provisions (§ 51-3-56) directing the
  trier of fact to apportion fault *under* § 51-12-33. The apportionment statute is untouched.
- § 51-12-33 as it currently stands: (g) a plaintiff "shall not be entitled to receive any
  damages if the plaintiff is 50 percent or more responsible"; (a) damages otherwise reduced
  in proportion to the plaintiff's own fault; (b) apportionment among liable parties is
  several, not joint, with no right of contribution; (c) the trier of fact considers the fault
  of nonparties, with (c)(1) requiring settlement with the nonparty or notice not later than
  120 days before trial.
- **The eight sections amend Titles 9, 40 and 51 only. None amends Title 34.** Workers'
  compensation is untouched by SB 68. (This negative claim is supported because the record
  enumerates the bill's complete section list from its own headers.)

### Forbidden — do not write these

**The single most common error in circulation, and the one to guard hardest against:**

> ❌ that SB 68 changed, clarified, tightened, reinforced or codified Georgia's comparative
> negligence rule, the 50% bar, or O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33.

**It did not.** Secondary reporting says SB 68 "clarified and reinforced" the 50% bar, which
reads as though the statute changed. The statute is untouched and the 2024 text is current.
Do not write it, do not imply it, and do not hedge toward it.

Also forbidden:

- ❌ Any statement that SB 68 changed a statute of limitations or filing deadline. It did not
  touch any deadline — that was checked directly.
- ❌ Describing SB 68 as "retroactive," full stop. The precise, verified position is: two
  sections are **prospective only**, and the rest are intended to reach causes of action
  **pending** on the effective date. "Applies to pending cases" is not "retroactive," and the
  distinction is the whole point of the reassurance in §3(a). Use the Section 9 language.
- ❌ Any characterization of what Section 6 *requires* for negligent security beyond "creates
  a cause of action and directs apportionment under § 51-12-33." The record gives the subject,
  not the substance. Do not write "stricter standard," "higher burden," or anything about what
  a plaintiff must now prove.
- ❌ Any characterization of what Section 7 does to medical special damages. In particular, do
  **not** write that plaintiffs may now show jurors only amounts actually paid rather than
  amounts billed. That is a competitor's characterization, not a verified fact. The verified
  fact is the section's subject: special damages for medical and healthcare expenses.
- ❌ Any mechanics of Section 5 beyond "seat-belt evidence is now admissible." Do not state
  how it may be used, whether it operates as a defense, what it does to damages, or any
  threshold or limit.
- ❌ Any characterization of Sections 1, 2, 3 or 4 beyond the subject line in the table.
- ❌ "Judicial hellhole," ATRA rankings, or any state-vs-state litigation-climate ranking.
- ❌ Punitive damages, damage caps, § 51-12-5.1 — not verified, and not in SB 68.
- ❌ Any prediction about outcomes, values, constitutional challenges, or judicial
  interpretation.

### SB 69 — verify or omit

SB 68's companion bill **SB 69** (third-party litigation funding) exists and is real —
`sb 69 georgia` returns 40/mo and multiple ranking pages pair the two bills. **But nothing
about its contents was verified this session.**

Two acceptable handlings, writer's choice:

1. **Omit it entirely.** Safest, costs almost nothing — 40/mo and decaying.
2. **Verify it first** against the signed text on `legis.ga.gov`, then write it, and add the
   verified facts to the verification record as a new section so the next page inherits them.

**Not acceptable:** describing SB 69 from memory, from a firm client alert, or from the
Reynolds/Zelle pages that pair it with SB 68. If it goes on the page unverified, the page
does not ship.

### Primary sources — a gotcha

`legis.ga.gov` bill landing pages (e.g. `/legislation/68204`) are a JavaScript SPA and render
as a loading message to any non-browser fetch. **Do not cite a landing page you have not seen
rendered.** The document endpoint used by the verification record does serve directly and is
the one to cite:

`https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/legislation/document/20252026/236944` — signed text, 19pp (200 ✓)

The Governor's signing announcement also resolves and carries the 2025-04-21 date:
`https://gov.georgia.gov/press-releases/2025-04-21/gov-kemp-signs-historic-legislation-delivering-commonsense-meaningful` (200 ✓)

## 7. Outline

House format: H2s phrased as questions, answer-first opening sentence under each, Key
Takeaways at top, FAQs at bottom. Target 1,600–2,000 words.

1. **`## What is Georgia's SB 68?`** (~200w) — Approved 2025-04-21, effective on approval,
   eight substantive sections across Titles 9, 40 and 51. Name the subjects. Answer in the
   first sentence.
2. **`## Does SB 68 apply to my case?`** (~350w) — **The most valuable section on the page.**
   Lead with it because it is the reader's actual question. Sections 6 and 7 apply only to
   causes of action arising on or after 2025-04-21; prior causes are governed by prior law.
   Everything else was intended to reach causes of action pending on the effective date.
   Use the Section 9 framing precisely; do not compress it into "retroactive."
3. **`## What SB 68 changed — section by section`** (~400w) — The table from §4's allowlist,
   plus prose at exactly the verified level of detail. Discipline here is the whole job: state
   the subject, do not editorialize the substance.
4. **`## Can the defense now bring up my seat belt?`** (~200w) — Section 5 amended
   § 40-8-76.1 and seat-belt evidence is now admissible. State it plainly; state that this is
   the change most likely to come up in an ordinary Georgia car-crash claim; **stop there.**
   Do not describe how it may be used or what it does to a recovery. Link
   `/practice-areas/car-accident-lawyers/`.
5. **`## Will my case be split into two trials?`** (~200w) — Section 8 added § 51-12-15
   (bifurcation) **with a carve-out under $150,000 in controversy.** Lead with the carve-out;
   it is the reassuring, verified, differentiating fact.
6. **`## Did SB 68 change the deadline to file or the 50 percent rule?`** (~150w) —
   **No, to both.** The corrective section. Keep it to ~120 words on § 51-12-33 and hand off
