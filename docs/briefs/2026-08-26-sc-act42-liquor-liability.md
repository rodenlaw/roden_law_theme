- No page ties joint and several liability to alcohol or intoxication at all.
- The three pages that pair "alcohol" with "exception" are about **Georgia

What exists is 25 pages describing South Carolina dram-shop liability accurately
but without the rule that now governs it. That is worth fixing. It is not an

## Allowlist — may be stated as written

### The Act

- **2025 Act No. 42 (H.3430)**, "Tort Reform and Liquor Liability."
- Signed by the Governor **12 May 2025**. Passed 105–0 in the House and 45–0 in
  the Senate.
- **Effective 1 January 2026**, and Section 11 provides that it "applies only to
  causes of action or claims arising or accruing after January 1, 2026, and
  applies to all policies issued after that date," except § 61-4-523 which took
  effect on approval.
- Eleven sections. These are the subjects, at this level of detail and no further:

### § 15-38-15 — what actually changed

**The 50% threshold in subsection (A) did not change.** Both the pre-2026 and
post-2026 versions read: joint and several liability "does not apply to any
defendant whose conduct is determined to be less than fifty percent of the total
fault." A defendant at exactly 50% remains jointly liable under both.

**Subsection (F), the exception, is what changed.** The code publishes both
versions with the note *"Section effective until January 1, 2026. See, also,
section 15-38-15 effective January 1, 2026."*

| | Until 2026-01-01 | From 2026-01-01 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | "This section does not apply…" | "The provisions of subsection (A) do not apply…" |
| Covers | wilful, wanton, reckless, **grossly negligent**, or intentional conduct, or conduct involving **the use, sale, or possession of alcohol** or the illegal or illicit use, sale, or possession of drugs | wilful, wanton, reckless, or intentional conduct, or conduct involving the illegal or illicit use, sale, or possession of **drugs** |
| Consequence | (not stated) | "and such a defendant shall be jointly and severally liable for all damages described in subsection (A)" |

So **alcohol and gross negligence come out of the carve-out**, the carve-out is
narrowed to subsection (A) only, and its consequence is now stated expressly.

New subsections **(G)** and **(H)** govern adding a nondefendant tortfeasor to the
verdict form — a 180-day disclosure requirement, burden of proof on the defendant,
and exclusions.

### § 61-2-147 — the new rule, quoted

> "(A) Notwithstanding the provisions of Section 15-38-15, a tortfeasor charged
> under Section 56-5-2930, 56-5-2933, or 56-5-2945 shall appear on the jury
> verdict form upon motion of the defendant, provided such motion is made within
> one hundred eighty days of the commencement of the action or at a later time for
> good cause shown. The plaintiff may also add the tortfeasor … as a party
> defendant with the amended pleading relating back to the commencement of the
> action. **If a verdict is rendered against both a licensee and a defendant
> charged under Section 56-5-2930, 56-5-2933, or 56-5-2945, then the licensee is
> jointly and severally liable for fifty percent of the plaintiff's actual
> damages.** (B) Notwithstanding the time requirement in subsection (A), a
> settling tortfeasor, whether or not a party, shall be added to the verdict form."

The cited criminal sections are DUI (§ 56-5-2930), driving with an unlawful
alcohol concentration (§ 56-5-2933), and felony DUI (§ 56-5-2945).

### § 61-2-145 — licensee insurance

Both versions require on-premises licensees open after 5:00 p.m. to carry at least
**$1,000,000** in liquor liability cover. From 2026 the figure is expressed as an
**annual aggregate**, with a **per-occurrence minimum of 50% of the aggregate**,
and an exemption for licensees qualifying under a **liquor liability risk
mitigation program** under subsection (E).

## Forbidden — do not write these

- ❌ **"Act 42 removed dram-shop liability"** or any suggestion that bars are no
  longer liable. It did not abolish anything. It changed how a licensee's share is
  calculated.
- ❌ **"Act 42 changed South Carolina's comparative negligence rule"** or the 51%
  bar. It did not. Subsection (A)'s 50% joint-and-several threshold is also
  unchanged. This is the same error that circulated about SB 68 in Georgia, and it
  will circulate about this Act too.
- ❌ **"The licensee is jointly and severally liable for 50% of damages"** stated
  flatly. The rule is conditional: it applies **where a verdict is rendered against
  both** a licensee and a defendant charged under one of the three DUI sections.
- ❌ Describing the change as purely good or purely bad for injured people. Removing
  alcohol from § 15-38-15(F) narrows one route to full joint liability;
  § 61-2-147 creates a different, specific one. Anyone claiming to know the net
  effect across all cases is guessing.
- ❌ Applying any of this to **Georgia**. Five site pages discuss dram-shop
  liability under O.C.G.A. § 51-1-40. Act 42 is South Carolina law and has nothing
  to do with them.
- ❌ Any characterisation of §§ 61-3-100 to 61-3-130 (server training), § 61-4-523,
  § 38-73-550 or the captive-insurance sections beyond the subjects in the table.
  Those were not read in full.
- ❌ Stating which version governs a particular reader's case. Section 11 turns on
  when the cause of action arose or accrued, which is a fact about their accident.
